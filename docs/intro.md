# Learnissimo

## Problem Statement

When learning from a book, a textbook, or exam material, a learner who tries to fully understand what they are reading is often simultaneously trying to memorize it, write it down, or build a conspect. Splitting attention this way gets in the way of understanding, and the resulting notes are still an imperfect record of what was actually understood or will be retained.

- Note-taking and summarizing while reading compete with comprehension for the same attention.
- Without a way to check retention afterward, the learner does not know which parts of the material need more work.

## Proposed Solution

Learnissimo takes over the memorization and verification work, so a learner can read for understanding alone. Tests are built for a specific section of a source — a chapter, a paragraph, an exam ticket — either generated automatically from the source or built from the learner's own brief notes fed into Learnissimo. Each test item is grounded in, and linked to, the exact place in the source it comes from.

- Tests are tailored to what a given learner finds important, not built to exhaustively cover everything a source could be tested on.
- Learnissimo effectively builds the conspect a learner would otherwise write by hand, but as references to specific points in the source paired with test entries grounded in those points, rather than as a summary.
- Taking a test shows the learner what is understood and what needs review, and every test item leads back to its source location on demand.
- Creating and modifying tests from a source works in fully automated mode, in semi-automated mode via natural-language requests to change something, or in manual mode where anything can be adjusted directly.
